    Lessons¶

    Hard-won notes on running VUMPS — behavior that is correct by implementation but easy to misread, and the practical consequences for how a run should be set up and judged. To be updated over time.

    The Environment Budget¶

    env_maxiter running out is the most common warning a VUMPS run produces, and the one most easily misread. It does not mean the fixed point is unreachable, and it usually does not mean the energy is wrong.

    What the residual measures¶

    The environment on each side is the fixed point of a regularized power iteration: a block is carried around the unit cell, the diverging channel is projected out, and the cycle repeats until the block stops moving. The number compared against env_tol is the change in the block over one cell, \(\lVert E_{k+1} - E_k \rVert\), and it falls geometrically,

    \[ \lVert E_{k+1} - E_k \rVert \sim \lambda_2^{\,k}, \]

    with \(\lambda_2\) the subleading eigenvalue of the MPS transfer operator. Writing env_tol as \(\epsilon\), the budget it needs therefore follows from the correlation length \(\xi = -1/\ln \lambda_2\), counted in unit cells rather than sites:

    \[ k \;\approx\; \xi \, \ln\frac{1}{\epsilon} \;\approx\; 28\,\xi \quad \text{at} \quad \epsilon = 10^{-12}. \]

    The default env_maxiter = 200 covers \(\xi\) up to about seven unit cells.

    Nothing stalls¶

    Measured residuals after a given number of absorbed cells, for states converged under the two-site scheme on a two-site cell, with the solver run to a fixed budget rather than to a tolerance:

    model D 25 cells 50 100 200 400 800
    Heisenberg S=½ (gapless) 32 3.2e-3 1.2e-3 1.9e-4 4.7e-6 2.9e-9 1.3e-15
    Majumdar-Ghosh (gapped) 8 4.8e-1 1.9e-1 3.1e-2 7.8e-4 5.1e-7 2.2e-13
    Majumdar-Ghosh (gapped) 32 1.2e-1 2.4e-3 9.2e-7 1.2e-13 — —
    Heisenberg S=1 (Haldane gap) 32 1.3e-9 6.5e-14 — — — —

    Every case reaches machine precision given enough cells. There is no residual floor short of it, so env_tol = 1e-12 is a meaningful default rather than an unreachable one, and the question is only ever how many cells it costs.

    Gapped models saturate with the bond dimension¶

    For a gapped state, \(\lambda_2\) converges to a physical value once the bond dimension is large enough to capture the correlation length, and widening the state further does not slow the iteration down. The Haldane chain, which is gapped with a unique ground state:

    D 5 cells 10 20 40 \(\lambda_2\) cells for 1e-12
    16 2.5e-2 2.6e-5 1.3e-7 7.9e-12 0.62 ~44
    32 2.8e-1 1.9e-3 5.9e-8 3.4e-12 0.61 ~43
    64 6.7e-1 2.5e-2 5.1e-6 1.6e-13 0.42 ~38
    128 1.1e+0 1.2e-1 8.7e-5 3.0e-11 0.48 ~45

    Flat across a factor of eight in D, at roughly a fifth of the default budget. A production run on a gapped model at a bond dimension in the hundreds is in the same position as this one at 16.

    A critical model has no such saturation. The correlation length of a uniform MPS at a critical point is cut off by its own bond dimension rather than by the physics, so \(\xi\) grows with D without bound, and so does the budget. On the Heisenberg spin-½ chain, \(\lambda_2\) measured on the converged state climbs from 0.82 at D = 8 (\(\xi \approx 5\) cells) to 0.91 at D = 16 (\(\xi \approx 11\)) to 0.96 at D = 32 (\(\xi \approx 28\)). The last of those needs around 800 cells for 1e-12, four times the default.

    Two gapped cases that still exhaust the budget¶

    A degenerate ground state closes the transfer-operator gap much as a critical point does. A state carrying far more bond dimension than its entanglement needs does the same for a numerical reason: the surplus Schmidt weights are noise, and they leave the transfer operator near-degenerate. Majumdar-Ghosh is both at once — spontaneously dimerized, so two-fold degenerate, and exactly representable at D = 2:

    D \(\lambda_2\) cells for 1e-12
    16 0.79 ~121
    32 0.86 ~187
    64 0.98 ~1200
    128 0.97 ~1040

    The rate gets worse as the state is widened past what it needs, which is the opposite of the Haldane behavior above. A warning on a model believed to be gapped is usually reporting one of these two situations, or saying that the model is closer to critical than assumed, and all three are worth knowing about rather than silencing.

    The Energy Is Not the Environment¶

    The energy converges long before the environment tensor it is extracted from. On the Heisenberg chain at D = 32, taking the environment at 100 cells — where the residual is still 1.9e-4, five orders of magnitude above the default tolerance — gives an energy that agrees with the fully converged one to 3e-14:

    cells residual energy error solve time
    100 1.9e-4 2.7e-14 0.20 s
    200 4.7e-6 1.4e-14 0.41 s
    400 2.9e-9 2.4e-15 0.83 s
    800 1.3e-15 reference 1.66 s

    The residual tracks the subleading tail of a tensor; the energy is a scalar contraction that converges at the leading rate. A run reporting an energy to the digit you care about, while warning on every iteration, is not contradicting itself.

    Where a loose environment does cost something

    A one-site run notices immediately, because its gradient compares quantities built from the environments and cannot be better than they are. Continuing a converged two-site state for 25 iterations under '1s' reached a gradient of 4.2e-5 at env_tol = 1e-6, 2.5e-5 at 1e-8, 1.7e-5 at 1e-10, and 1.2e-5 at 1e-12. A run that reports its gradient under '1s' wants a tight tolerance, and env_maxiter raised with it.

    What it costs to raise the budget¶

    Absorbing one site into an environment and applying the effective Hamiltonian to a center tensor are the same contraction: a \(\chi \times \chi\) block carried through one site tensor and one iMPO tensor, step_left_env and matvec_center differing only in which index is left open. Both cost \(O(\chi^3 d w)\) densely, with a subleading \(O(\chi^2 d^2 w^2)\), for bond dimension \(\chi\), physical dimension \(d\), and iMPO width \(w\). Measured on the spin-1 chain under U(1), per call:

    D environment step \(H_{AC}\) matvec 50-cell solve
    64 1.3 ms 1.4 ms 0.15 s
    128 1.7 ms 1.7 ms 0.19 s
    256 2.9 ms 3.2 ms 0.32 s
    512 6.8 ms 7.7 ms 0.77 s
    1024 33 ms 38 ms 3.5 s
    2048 109 ms 117 ms 11.5 s

    The two columns stay within a tenth of each other across a factor of 32 in width. Both grow by roughly four per doubling rather than the eight a dense contraction would give, because the symmetry splits the tensors into many small blocks and the block count rises with the bond dimension.

    The consequence is the useful part: the environment's share of an iteration is set by counts, not by the bond dimension. An iteration spends \(2 k N\) environment steps, for \(k\) cells absorbed on each side of a cell of \(N\) sites, against roughly one Davidson matvec per iteration of each local solve. Exhaust a 200-cell budget and the environments dominate the iteration; converge in twenty and they are a minor term. That balance is the same at \(\chi = 64\) and at \(\chi = 2048\), so the budget is worth deciding on the correlation length of the model rather than on the size of the calculation. The loop exits on env_tol, so a budget raised for safety costs nothing on the runs that do not need it.

    Choosing the Two Settings¶

    Ordered by what the run is for:

    • A gapped model, any bond dimension. The defaults are sized for this and need no attention. If the warning appears anyway, read it as a statement about the state: degenerate, over-wide, or less gapped than expected.
    • A critical model, energy wanted. Loosen env_tol to what the solver actually reaches within the budget, around 1e-8 on the chains in the examples. The energy is unchanged and the output stays readable.
    • A critical model, gradient wanted, or a one-site run. Keep env_tol tight and raise env_maxiter instead, to roughly \(28\xi\) with \(\xi\) estimated from a short scan of the residual against the budget.
