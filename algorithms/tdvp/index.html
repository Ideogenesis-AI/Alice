    TDVP¶

    Alice's TDVP algorithm evolves an MPS in real time under a Hamiltonian MPO, |ψ(t)⟩ = exp(-i H t) |ψ(0)⟩, by projecting the Schrödinger equation onto the tangent space of the MPS manifold at the current state.

    API¶

    Symbol Description
    Options Run options: scheme, time step, step count, integrator order, bond dimension, bond expansion, caching, artifacts
    Summary Evolution history: times, energies, norms, bond dimensions, discarded weights
    Artifact State snapshot at one time step
    run Top-level entry point

    Usage Pattern¶

    from alice.network import build_interaction, build_hamiltonian, init_mps
from alice.algorithm import tdvp

interactions, spc, geo = build_interaction(cfg)
H = build_hamiltonian(interactions, geo.L, spc)

state = init_mps(geo.L, spc, ops, bond_dim=1, config=[1, 0, 1, 0])

opts = tdvp.Options(scheme='2s', dt=0.05, n_steps=40, max_bond=64)
summary, artifact = tdvp.run(tdvp.Artifact(state=state, time=0.0, step=0), H, opts)

for t, e in zip(summary.times, summary.energies):
    print(f"t = {t:.4f},  E = {e:.10f}")

    run() takes the initial state as an Artifact (state, time, step) and the Hamiltonian MPO as a separate argument — unlike XTRG, where the generator is baked into the density matrix being squared.

    A real-valued input MPS is promoted to complex128 in place, logged at INFO. Evolution is unitary, so there is nothing to gain from a real state and nothing correct to be had from one.

    Resuming and continuing¶

    Resuming an interrupted run is calling run() again with the Artifact loaded from tdvp.ckpt; the time and energy history is recovered from evolution.ckpt automatically:

    resumed = tdvp.Artifact.load(ckpt_dir / 'tdvp.ckpt')
summary, artifact = tdvp.run(resumed, H, opts)

    A finished run continues from any archived artifacts/step_XXXXX.ckpt with new options — here evolving from t = 2.0 out to t = 5.0 at a larger bond dimension:

    state = tdvp.Artifact.load(ckpt_dir / 'artifacts' / 'step_00040.ckpt')
opts = tdvp.Options(dt=0.05, n_steps=100, max_bond=256,
                    checkpoint_dir=str(ckpt_dir))
summary, artifact = tdvp.run(state, H, opts)

    n_steps is the absolute step index to stop at, counted from t = 0, not a number of additional steps, and dt must match the step the recovered history was built on. Starting from a step before the end of the history truncates it back to that step (with a warning) and recomputes forward, overwriting both evolution.ckpt and the affected step_XXXXX.ckpt archives.

    Not every step has to be archived. save_artifacts_every sets the stride, and because it is keyed on the absolute step index, a resumed run writes the same grid as an uninterrupted one. A run also stops on a grid point rather than between two, so its final state is always among the snapshots; see Artifact.

    Update Schemes¶

    Name Alias Description
    '1s' '1-site', 'one-site' 1-site update; cannot change bond dimension
    '2s' '2-site', 'two-site' 2-site update with SVD truncation; grows bonds as entanglement develops
    '1sp' '1-site-plus', 'one-site-plus' Update on a bond widened beforehand by controlled bond expansion; grows bonds with SVD truncation as '2s' does, at close to 1-site cost

    The 1-site scheme cannot grow bonds

    This is a sharper restriction than the corresponding one in DMRG or XTRG, where a 1-site run merely converges less well. The 1-site TDVP manifold is one of fixed bond dimension, so a quench started from a product state — every bond of dimension 1 — stays a product state forever, while norm and energy remain conserved to machine precision and the run looks healthy.

    run() detects this and warns at startup, naming the offending bonds:

    scheme '1s' cannot grow bond dimensions, and bond(s) 0, 4 have dimension
1; no correlation can cross them, so the dynamics there will be frozen
while norm and energy stay conserved. Use scheme '2s' or '1sp', or warm up
with one of them until every bond has opened.

    Start from a state whose bonds are already open, or use '2s' (the default) or '1sp'.

    Controlled Bond Expansion¶

    '1sp' widens the bond ahead of each update before evolving over it, so the rank adapts as it does under '2s' while every local exponential stays 1-site sized. The widening comes from the complement of the space the bond currently spans: H is applied to a cheaply factored two-site tensor, the part of the result the current bond already covers is projected out, and up to expand_k directions are taken from what is left. Those are the directions the next step can flow into. The split that follows truncates the bond back under max_bond/trunc_thresh, as in '2s'.

    Option Meaning
    expand_k Cap on the directions added to a bond per local update. A typical choice is round(sqrt(max_bond)); 0 reduces the scheme to '1s'
    expand_alpha Internal bond of the cheap factorization, which is what keeps the search below 2-site cost. A typical choice is round(sqrt(max_bond)), as for expand_k; None keeps the full bond

    expand_k is a cap rather than a request. A bond gains however many directions its complement actually holds, which is often fewer: directions whose singular value falls below the scale the projection cancelled on are discarded, because they are products of that cancellation and do not lie in the complement at all. For a short-range Hamiltonian the genuine count is small — one or two per update on the free-fermion benchmarks — so raising expand_k past it changes nothing. A bond profile still climbs steadily, since the cap applies to one bond in one half-sweep rather than to the trajectory.

    The scheme escapes the frozen-product-state trap above: a bond of dimension 1 has a complement like any other, so a quench from a product state evolves. On the melting domain wall it matches '2s' to 9e-05 against the exact Gaussian solution while carrying a smaller bond profile, since it only ever adds directions the complement offers.

    '1sp' is not exactly time-reversible

    The expansion is chosen from the state it starts out in, so a forward step and a backward step do not widen the bond into the same subspace, and reversing a run does not return the initial state to roundoff the way '1s' and '2s' do. The deviation enters at the order of the integrator error and falls as dt², so it is bounded by the accuracy already being asked for; use '2s' where exact reversibility is the point of the calculation.

    Integrator Order¶

    order Sweep structure
    1 a single half-sweep at dt, alternating direction between steps (Lie-Trotter)
    2 a forward half-sweep at dt/2 then a backward one, leaving the center back at site 0 (Lubich symmetric)

    Both are globally second order at equal cost, because run() alternates the direction for order=1: two consecutive order=1 steps of size dt are exactly one order=2 step of size 2dt — the same local exponentials in the same sequence, verified to 1e-9.

    The difference is where the snapshots land. Every order=2 step ends a complete forward-and-backward sweep pair, whereas an order=1 snapshot taken after an odd step carries first-order error. If observables are measured at every step rather than only at the end, prefer order=2.

    Note that error scaling in dt is only observable on a restricted manifold. At full bond dimension the tangent-space projector is the identity, TDVP is exact at any step size, and halving dt changes nothing.

    The Tangent-Space Projection¶

    TDVP replaces d|ψ⟩/dt = -i H |ψ⟩ with its projection onto the tangent space of the manifold of MPS at fixed bond dimension:

    \[ P_{T_\psi} = \sum_n P^{L}_{<n} \otimes \mathbb{1}_n \otimes P^{R}_{>n} - \sum_n P^{L}_{\leq n} \otimes P^{R}_{>n} \]

    The algorithm never forms this operator. The first sum is the sequence of single-site evolutions over the sweep, each of which is the projection onto one of its terms: the left and right environments built from canonical neighbors are exactly P^L and P^R, so exp(-i H_eff dt) acting on one site tensor moves the state only within that term's image.

    The second sum, with its minus sign, is the zero-site back-evolution on each bond, applied with -dt. It removes the double counting between consecutive terms of the first sum. Omitting it does not merely degrade accuracy — it breaks even first-order consistency.

    flowchart LR
    evolveSite["evolve M_i by exp(-i H1_i dt)"] --> qrSplit["QR: left isometry + bond tensor"]
    qrSplit --> backEvolve["evolve bond by exp(+i H0_i+1 dt)"]
    backEvolve --> absorb["absorb bond into M_i+1"]

    One full sweep is one time step. There is no inner convergence loop and no e_tol analogue: TDVP integrates, it does not optimize.

    Local Exponentials¶

    Each local update exponentiates an effective Hamiltonian against one tensor with a Lanczos–Krylov method (krylov.py), using full re-orthogonalization and exponentiating the Lanczos tridiagonal exactly via numpy.linalg.eigh. It terminates on happy breakdown or when the last Krylov coefficient falls below krylov_tol; Summary.krylov_errors records the per-step maximum of that estimate, and a run whose values sit near krylov_tol wants a larger krylov_dim.

    Conserved Quantities¶

    Energy and norm are conserved exactly by the projected flow, which makes them diagnostics of numerical health rather than of physical accuracy — a run that has truncated away the real physics still conserves both. Trust them to detect a broken integrator, not a converged answer. The quantities to converge in max_bond are the observables themselves.

    renormalize defaults to False so that norm loss from truncation stays visible in Summary.norms.
